Phage display technique is the traditional method for antibody maturation. We developed the HAAS platform to break the limitation of the phage method.

HAAS came from the concept of "hot spots," as described by Clarkson and Wells in 1995. Hot spots contribute to most of the binding affinity within larger protein-protein binding interfaces.

The data from antibody protein data banks have proved that antigen and antibody interactions involve several hot-spot amino acids only. HAAS will limit the total number of amino acid substitutions required to improve the antibody affinity. The Opdivo amino acids' hotspots binding to PD-1 is shown on the right figure.

* A three-dimensional molecular model for the antibody is generated.

* The hot-spot amino acids are selected.

* The vectors with the mutation at the hot-spot are constructed.

* The antibodies are produced with the heavy chain and light chain mutants recombination.

* The mutant affinities are directly measured with these natural full length antibodies.

A phage display maturation library was constructed for a mouse antibody, with a kd at nM grade. No high affinity clones were found after the screening.

With the HAAS technique, 3 hotspot amino acids were selected. Only 27 recombined antibodies were constructed and transiently expressed with cos-7 cell. One high affinity mutant was found within the 27 antibodies. The maturation antibody affinity improved by more than 15 folds compared to the chimeric antibody as shown in above figure.
